**Short answer:** meet somewhere **public and busy**, **get there and back under your own steam**, and **tell someone** where you're going and when you'll check in. Keep your phone charged, keep the first date short, and don't share your home address until you're sure. If you feel uncomfortable in a pub or bar, **ask staff for "Angela"** and they'll help you leave.

This guide has no affiliate links. The advice comes from police, Get Safe Online and the dating sites' own safety pages.[^1]

## Before the date

- **Check they're real.** A short video call before you meet is the simplest test. If they keep finding reasons not to, treat it as a warning. The [romance scam guide]({{ '/romance-scams-over-50-warning-signs/' | relative_url }}) explains why.
- **Do a quick search.** Look up their name, and run a reverse image search on their photos to make sure they aren't someone else's.
- **Tell a friend or family member** who you're meeting, where, and when. Share the person's profile name and a photo.
- **Agree a check-in time**, and a code word that means "call me with an excuse to leave".
- **Charge your phone** and turn on location sharing with your trusted person for the evening if you're comfortable doing so.

## Choosing the place

- **Public and busy:** a café, a pub at lunchtime, a garden centre, a museum.
- **Somewhere you know**, close to your own transport, not theirs.
- **Daytime is easier.** It's natural to keep a coffee to an hour, and it gives you both an easy way to say "let's do this again".
- **Avoid their home or yours** until you know them well.

## During the date

- **Get yourself there and home.** Don't accept a lift on a first date, and don't let them see where you live.
- **Keep an eye on your drink**, and don't leave it unattended.
- **Keep personal details back**: your address, workplace, finances and family details can wait.
- **Trust your instincts.** You can leave at any point, for any reason, without explaining.

### Ask for Angela

Many pubs, bars and venues take part in Ask for Angela. If you feel unsafe or uncomfortable on a date, go to the bar and ask for "Angela". Staff will help you discreetly, for example by moving you somewhere out of sight, calling you a taxi, or asking the other person to leave. If you're at risk, they'll call the police.[^2]

If you're ever in immediate danger, call **999**.

## After the date

- **Message your check-in person** to say you're home safe.
- **Take your time.** Someone pressing for a quick second date at your home, or for money, is a red flag.
- **Report anyone who made you feel unsafe** to the dating site, so they can act, and to the police if they threatened or hurt you.

## Checking a partner's history: Clare's Law

As a relationship develops, you have a right to ask. In England and Wales, the **Domestic Violence Disclosure Scheme**, known as Clare's Law, lets you ask the police whether a new or current partner has a history of domestic abuse or violence.[^3] Friends and family can also apply if they're worried about you.

The police check their records, and a local panel decides what can safely be shared. Anything disclosed is confidential. If you or someone else is in immediate danger, call 999 rather than waiting.

## Questions people ask

### Where should I go on a first date?

Somewhere public and busy that you can reach and leave on your own, such as a café or a pub at lunchtime.

### What is Ask for Angela?

A scheme in many pubs and bars: ask staff for "Angela" if you feel unsafe on a date, and they'll help you leave discreetly.

### Can I check if a new partner has a history of abuse?

Yes. In England and Wales, Clare's Law lets you ask the police.
