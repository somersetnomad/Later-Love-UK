Romance Scams After 50: Warning Signs and What to Do

Romance fraud cost UK victims over £102 million last year, and people aged 55 to 74 lost the most. The warning signs, and what to do if it happens.

Bala Danmeri

Short answer: the clearest warning sign is being asked for money by someone you’ve only met online, for any reason. Others are moving fast to declarations of love, wanting to leave the dating site for private messages, always having a reason not to meet or video call, and asking you to keep the relationship secret. If any of this is happening, stop sending money, tell someone you trust, and report it.

This guide has no affiliate links. It’s here because romance fraud hits people over 50 hardest, and knowing the pattern is the best protection.

How big the problem is

Report Fraud, the UK’s national fraud reporting service, says romance fraud cost victims more than £102 million last year, from 10,784 reports, a 29% rise on the year before.1

The losses fall hardest on people in later life. People aged 55 to 74 lost the most, nearly half of the total. The average victim lost about £9,500, and some lost up to £1 million.

Romance scammers are organised and patient. They often build trust over weeks or months before money is mentioned, and the request usually comes wrapped in an emotional story, so the warning signs below matter more than how careful you think you are.

The warning signs

Police and Get Safe Online, the UK’s public online-safety service, point to the same pattern.2

They ask for money

This is the one that matters most. It might be a medical emergency, a business problem, a fee to release an inheritance, travel costs to come and see you, or an “investment” they want to share with you. Never send money or bank details to someone you’ve only met online, however convincing the reason and however long you’ve been talking.

Things move very fast

Strong declarations of love within days or weeks, talk of a future together before you’ve met, and lots of attention and flattery. Taking things slowly is a safety measure, not a sign of coldness.

They want to leave the dating site

Scammers often ask to move to WhatsApp, text or email early on. Staying on the dating site’s messaging keeps you inside its reporting and moderation tools.3

They can’t meet or video call

Someone who’s always abroad, working on an oil rig, serving overseas or just about to visit, but never quite does, and whose camera is always broken. Be particularly wary of people who say they’re local but turn out to be overseas.

They want secrecy

Asking you not to tell friends or family about them is a classic way to isolate you from people who’d spot the problem.

Their photos or story don’t add up

Pictures that look professional or too perfect, or details that change. Search their name and phrases from their messages along with words like “romance scam”, and run a reverse image search on their photos. Scammers often use pictures stolen from someone else.

Protect yourself before you start

  • Use a reputable dating site and keep messages on it until you’re sure the person is genuine.
  • Keep personal details back: your full name, date of birth, home address, workplace, and family details.
  • Talk about who you’re meeting with a friend or family member. A second opinion is the most effective protection there is.
  • Meet in person, in a public place, sooner rather than later. Someone who keeps avoiding meeting is a warning in itself. The first date safety checklist covers how.

If you think it’s happening to you

  1. Stop sending money straight away, however much it hurts to do so.
  2. Call your bank using the number on the back of your card, and tell them what’s happened. Do it quickly; the sooner they know, the better the chance of stopping payments.
  3. Report it. In England, Wales and Northern Ireland, report to Report Fraud at reportfraud.police.uk or on 0300 123 2040. In Scotland, call Police Scotland on 101.4
  4. Report the profile to the dating site, so they can remove it and protect others.
  5. Keep everything: messages, photos, payment records and usernames.
  6. Talk to someone. Victims often feel ashamed, and scammers rely on that. These are organised criminals who do this for a living. Being targeted says nothing about your judgement.

If someone threatens you or you feel in danger, call 999.

Questions people ask

What is the biggest sign of a romance scam?

Being asked for money by someone you’ve only met online, whatever the reason.

Who do I report a romance scam to?

Report Fraud (reportfraud.police.uk, 0300 123 2040) in England, Wales and Northern Ireland, or Police Scotland on 101. Tell your bank and the dating site too.

Are over-50s more likely to be targeted?

People aged 55 to 74 suffered the highest losses last year, nearly half of the £102 million total.
